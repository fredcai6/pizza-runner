# Table-read notes: Nintendo localisation room

Short version: Springfield has the best spine and the best single lines. Cop-show has the best "undercover" pun and the best character-driven door lines. Adventure has the best rebuttal beats. I still own the pseudo-patriotic speech (nobody else wrote one), Stan's "Is any of this worth it?" motive, and Phil's "A large fraction, but a fraction."

## Scene 1: Opening to New York

**Best beats**
- **Mamma mia placement.** Springfield, adventure and cop-show all put it straight after "Slices are vanishing all over the world."
  - That is the line it reacts to. Mine hung it on a "postcard" joke two boxes later, so I concede the placement.
  - Springfield's "**Pipe down**, Contessa. I'm getting to the string." is the best follow-up. It is a character beat and it feeds "pulling the strings."
- **Why "undercover."** Cop-show: "You go in under cover. Lid down. Nobody looks twice at a lid." It is the only draft that makes "undercover" a pun with a premise. I concede it.
- **The name "The Order."** Two good options.
  - Cop-show: "Printed right on the ticket. That's how good they are." The premise is visible to the player and he treats "they aren't hiding" as proof.
  - Springfield: "I came up with it myself. Nobody else says it. That's how deep it goes."
  - I'd take the ticket logic with Springfield's last clause. My own "an **order**" version was the same idea with less punch.
- **Deep undercover.** Adventure's "A slice that stops calling is **deep undercover**" is the cleanest. Mine ("The best don't come back at all... Silence is how you know it's working") is the same rule, a bit wordier.
- **Intros.**
  - Cop-show's "Three names. Only a senator needs all three." is the best Phil intro. Adventure's "Three names, because one would not hold him" is a close second.
  - Springfield's "Largest slice in the free world. Structural integrity, none." is the best straight-man line.
- **Vinny and the send-off.** Splitting "that's a **Tuesday** where I'm from. I ain't scared a nothin'." and "I fold for nobody." into two boxes (Springfield, cop-show) gives the setup and the punchline. Then Springfield's "New York first. Nobody looks twice at a loud one." / "Wait, what?" / "**Lid's open. Get in the box.**" is the best exit of the four drafts. It is one line, and it answers "Wait, what?" without saying what happens.

**Lines that don't work**
- Adventure: "Plain cheese, every one of you. Nobody suspects plain cheese." Stan is deep dish and Contessa is margherita, so the premise fails on the next card.
- Springfield: "if the tape goes on twice, that's deep undercover. Bottom of the stack." It needs three beats of explanation and "No contact. Ever." reads as ominous. Simpler is better.
- Cop-show: Contessa's "Someone puts my name on a box?" (scene 4). The box says "Original Italian," not her name, so the premise doesn't hold.
- Mine: "Thickest stack of tickets on the board" is a flat reason for New York. Springfield's and cop-show's reasons are better.

## Scene 2: New York returns to Chicago

**Best beats**
- **Vinny's report.** Cop-show's "Teenagers. Turtles. Somethin' in a drain. I got my picture took four times." sets up the board in one breath. I'd keep the liked "some a me didn't come back" after it.
  - Adventure's "one a me walked in a door and he ain't called" leans on deep undercover, but it rewrites Tommy's liked line. I'd leave his line alone.
- **The skeptic beat.** Springfield's "Has anybody tried? Provin' it?" / "That's how they get you." The conspiracy is not debunked, and the unfalsifiable reply is the joke.
- **The board.** Everyone kept "Slow is what they want you to think. It is also what they are." That is the best board line, so I'm restoring it. My cut of it was wrong.
- **Chicago.** Springfield's "I'm a dish. I don't dig." / "Dig anyway." / "...Fine." beats my "Second." / "Second City." Mine is a good rhythm but a thin reason.
  - **Alt:** OLD: "Chicago's next. I need something that digs." / DEEP: "I'm a dish." / OLD: "Second City. Dig." / DEEP: "...Fine."

**Lines that don't work**
- The "Read the **corkboard**" instruction appears in three drafts. It is a tutorial line, so keep one and trim the "It may save your crust" tail.

## Scene 3: Deep Dish returns to DC

**Best beats**
- **Stan's motive.** Mine only: "Is any of this worth it?" It is what the rally speech answers, and nobody else gives the speech a reason.
- **The speech.** Springfield's "A whole pie, shoulder to shoulder in the box. Lid down. Cozy. They will never know your name." Springfield dropped the number, and I concede that. City pies are six-slice and the finale is eight, so my "Eight to a box" and the adventure and cop-show "Eight slices" all contradict the six.
- **The pseudo-patriotic button.** Nobody else kept it, and Tommy's outline asks for it.
  - **Alt:** "One box, under cheese. **Indivisible!**" It has no number, and the joke is that a pizza is the most divisible thing there is.
- **"They sleep warm because you stand out there in the cold!"** is in three drafts, and it's the best line in the speech.
- **Aliases.**
  - Springfield's "Popular. Like a password is popular."
  - Adventure's "That is not a name. That is a cover with a typo."
  - Both win without debunking him. I'd use adventure's line and give Springfield's to the next push.
- **Phil goes to DC.** Cop-show's "Phil, nobody can turn you away. You're the size of a door." is better than my "You'll blend right in." It is a reason that follows from Phil.

**Lines that don't work**
- Springfield's "And the top is Washington." Flat, and it has no joke.
- Adventure's "You cannot sneak in, Phil, so do not." Contradictory phrasing.

## Scene 4: Jumbo returns to Naples

**Best beats**
- **Phil's report.**
  - Mine: "A **large** fraction, but a fraction."
  - Springfield: "I entered that city whole. The record shows nothing. It is classified."
  - Cop-show: "But I read every lid in the capital." Phil as the one slice who reads the fine print is a good character beat.
- **Contessa.** Springfield's "Everybody knows where I'm from. I tell them." is all of her in one line. My "I am the original!" is its louder cousin.
- **The Old Timer's leap.** Adventure's "Nothing honest announces itself." / "Honest things announce themselves **constantly**. This is Italy." is the best exchange in the whole scene. The pushback doesn't debunk him, and it makes her a suspect.
- **Closing.** Springfield's "...Finalmente. Somebody takes me home." makes the homesick note land.
- **Stan's aside.** Mine: "Mine too. I figured it was a font."

**Lines that don't work**
- Cop-show's "caro". It is off-voice for a woman who calls America "America."

## Scene 5: Battered team to Long Beach

**Which bridge is best.** Combine Springfield's fork with my "home" line.
- Springfield: "Twelve customers who can't spell it. One who can." / "Either she's the most honest customer in America, or she wrote the others' names."
  - Both readings are paranoid, and neither is proof.
  - Stan's "Or she's just a person who can spell." is turned back into suspicion by "That's exactly what she'd want you to think."
- Mine: "Twelve disguises. Then one real name. You only drop the disguise at **home**." It is a clean cap. The irony is that it actually is her home, which makes it the strongest hidden-truth line.
- Cop-show's "Nobody spells it right unless it's **theirs**." is good, but "every one a Katelyn" asserts more than he knows.
- Adventure's "Each one is a copy of a copy of a copy" and "Does it end, or did you run out?" / "They would expect that question" is the best skeptic exchange. It is charming, but the "string ends at one pin" metaphor is weaker than the spelling logic.
- My "One of each. It's a party." note is the one beat unique to my draft. It is dramatic irony, because the player knows it is a party order. Keep it as a sting.

**Order of the beats.** Fork, then "home" cap, then the "party" note.

**The closer.** Springfield's "You never leave the building." / "I left the building." / "When?" / "...1987." sets up the stale line better than anyone. Adventure's "I would love to, kid. I have been in this box a long time." gives one sincere beat before it. Cop-show's "Somebody has to mind the board" is weak because it lets him off the hook too easily.
