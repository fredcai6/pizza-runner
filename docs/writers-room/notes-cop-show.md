# Table-read notes: cop-show parody room

Tommy said nothing is sacred, so these notes treat all four drafts as fair game, mine included. A few of my own lines are marked "mine" where I'm cutting them. Departures from his notes are flagged where they come up.

## The big call: the "deep undercover" premise

The silence-as-success premise beats the lid-as-cover premise as the engine. That covers adventure-game and Nintendo, and Springfield partly. Mine put "deep undercover" in CITY_LOST only, so Scene 1 never sets it up.

- **Nintendo, best statement:** "Most come back with a report. The best don't come back at all. That is **deep undercover**." It is a clean rule, and the Old Timer's reading is wrong in a way the player can see.
- **Adventure-game, best deployment:** "And the one who has not called? **Deep undercover.** Be proud." This pays off Vinny's missing slice in Scene 2.
- **Springfield, the "tape goes on twice / bottom of the stack" version, doesn't hold.** The stack is never established, and the player can't track it. Keep Springfield's "Lid's open. Get in the box." only if it stays a bare cover joke.
- **Adventure-game's "Plain cheese, every one of you. Nobody suspects plain cheese. That is your cover."** is the best cover line in the room. It callbacks "warm, plain, and full of hope", and it is better than my "Nobody looks twice at a lid".
- **Recommended stack:** plain cheese is the cover, silence is the success metric, and the tape on the board labelled DEEP UNDERCOVER is the payoff.

## Scene 5: Long Beach bridge

**Best bridge: Nintendo's.**
- "Twelve disguises. Then one real name. You only drop the disguise at **home**."
- It is his interpretation and not proof. It explains why he points to this house. It makes the correct spelling the clue. It turns on "alias", the game's own word.
- Springfield's "Either she's the most honest customer in America, or she wrote the others' names" is a better skeptic setup than anyone else's.
- Springfield's pair works as the skeptic exchange: Stan's "Or she's just a person who can spell." / "That's exactly what she'd want you to think." Mine had Vinny and Phil say this too. Pick one skeptic.

**Doesn't work**
- **Mine:** "Nobody spells it right unless it's theirs" is incoherent, since anyone can spell it right. Concede to Nintendo.
- **Adventure-game's "ran out of string":** this is the version Tommy called weakest, with a new coat of paint. "Does it end, or did you run out?" / "They would expect that question" is funny, but it doesn't bridge. Keep it as a Stan aside if the bridge is Nintendo's.
- **Nintendo's "The note says 'One of each. It's a party.'":** check the game actually shows such a note before we lean on it. If not, drop it.
- **Nintendo's Naples line:** "That is almost logical" is a mild nod toward validation. It is acceptable as a skeptic aside because it doesn't confirm him.

**The ending exchange**
- Springfield's "You never leave the building." / "I left the building." / "When?" / "...1987." is the funniest and sets up the swear.
- Nintendo: "Me? Kid, I was in the oven before your grandparents were **dough**." Fine, but less specific.
- Mine: "Somebody has to mind the board." This gives a clear motive. Splice it in:
  - Stan: "You never leave the building."
  - Old Timer: "I left the building."
  - Stan: "When?"
  - Old Timer: "...Somebody has to mind the board."
  - Old Timer: "I'm too stale for this shit."
  - 1987 can move to a dossier or a pan.
