# Pizza Runner script pass: Nintendo localisation room

## 1. Voice sheet

**The Old Timer.** He talks like a drill sergeant who has read too many case files. Sentences are short and ordered, and he is certain of everything. He wants the recruits to stay brave and to keep feeding his board, and underneath he wants someone to be out there at all. Tic: "Think about it." He drops it as a one-beat order and never explains it. He turns every mundane fact into a clue, and he is warmest when he is angriest ("Kid, I know the feeling").

**Vinny Two-Fold (NY).** Bravado that leaks. He drops his g's, calls everyone "boss," and says he is not scared as a way of saying that he is. Tic: "I ain't sayin' X. I'm sayin' Y," where Y is the confession. He wants respect, and what he has is nerve.

**Big Stan (Chicago).** He is laconic and deadpan, and he defends himself before anyone attacks. He wants to be taken seriously as a pizza, and he would like to be left alone. Tic: the pre-emptive "It's not a casserole," and a flat "...Fine." He is slow in speech the way he is slow on the ground, and the pauses are the jokes.

**La Contessa (Naples).** She is imperious and theatrical, and she has been offended by America since she arrived. She wants her certification acknowledged. She drops articles ("Is not X, is **margherita**") and counts seconds, because ninety in the fire is her whole religion. Tic: "Mamma mia," rationed. She is sincere under the grandeur and goes soft only about home.

**Phil A. Buster (DC).** He speaks in the Senate: "if I may," "let the record show," "I yield." His sentences start grand and get cut off mid-word by the Old Timer. He wants the floor and means every word. Tic: he asks to yield his time to himself. He treats being battered as a procedural matter.

## 2. Old Timer name candidates

1. **Sgt. Gus Dayold.** It sounds like a real cop, and it pairs with "I'm too stale for this shit." My pick.
2. **Lou Tenant.** It is the cheap pun, but the police hat sells it, and people mishear it as "Lieutenant."
3. **Sarge Reggiano.** Hard, aged and grated by life, like the cheese.
4. **Sal Ami.** A cop name that hides the topping, which fits a slice who thinks he is hiding things.
5. **Chief Ricotta.** Soft-sounding and wrong for a man with that mustache, which is a joke in itself. It works as a nickname the recruits use behind his back.

## 3. The five briefing scenes

### Scene 1: Opening to New York

**LOGIC:** He has tapped the dispatch line and noticed that every vanishing begins with a ticket, an order. So the party behind it is The Order (his pun on "order"). "Deep undercover" gets a working premise: the best agents never report back, so silence proves success. He sends New York first because New York has the thickest stack of tickets, and Vinny's bravado volunteers him.

**COMP wide**
- OLD: New recruits. Look at you. **Warm**, plain, and full of hope. [T]
- OLD: Welcome to Pizza HQ. [T]

**COMP board**
- OLD: Slices are vanishing all over the world. [T]
- OLD: Whole pies, gone in a night. Some nights all that turns up is the crust.
- OLD: Someone is pulling the strings. [T]
- OLD: I tapped the dispatch line. Every vanishing starts the same way. A ticket prints. An **order**.
- OLD: So that is my name for them. The **Order**. Nobody else uses it. Think about it.

**COMP wide**
- OLD: So you go out as operatives. Cardboard cover. Nobody looks twice at a pizza.
- OLD: Most come back with a report. The best don't come back at all. That is **deep undercover**.
- OLD: No calls. No postcards. Silence is how you know it's working.

**COMP naples**
- NAPLES: Mamma mia! Not a word? Not even a postcard? In Napoli we send postcards.
- OLD: **Pipe down**, Contessa. Silence is the point. Let's do the roster.

**COMP naples** (card: La Contessa / Margherita of Naples)
- OLD: La Contessa. Fastest slice in the oven. Certified a pizza by an actual government.
- NAPLES: Ninety seconds in the fire. You would not last **nine**.

**COMP deep** (card: Big Stan / Deep Dish of Chicago)
- OLD: Big Stan. Two inches of sauce, four of grudge. Slow to start. Slower to stop.
- DEEP: It's not a casserole.
- OLD: Nobody said it was.
- DEEP: Somebody will.

**COMP jumbo** (card: Phil A. Buster / Jumbo Slice of Washington)

- JUMBO: Thank you. I would like to yield my time to myself, for brief remarks on the subject of--

**COMP ny** (card: Vinny Two-Fold / Slice of New York)

- NY: Disappearances? Boss, that's a **Tuesday** where I'm from. I fold for nobody.

### Scene 2: New York returns, then Chicago

**LOGIC:** Vinny's fear becomes intel, because "some didn't come back" is exactly what deep undercover predicts. The board's dossiers are the observations. The Old Timer's "agents of The Order" is the interpretation. Chicago is next because it is the second-thickest stack of tickets, and "Second City" gives Stan a grievance to carry out the door.

**COMP ny**
- NY: Boss, it's hell out there. [T]
- NY: And I ain't sayin' I'm rattled. I'm sayin' some a me didn't come back. [T]
- OLD: Some of the best don't. These jobs aren't for the **limp crusted**. [T]
- OLD: But you did, and you brought **intel**. Intel goes on the board.

**COMP board** (pan begins)

**COMP boardTeen**

**COMP board**

### Scene 3: Deep Dish returns, then DC

**LOGIC:** Stan asks whether any of this is worth it, and that gives the rah-rah speech a motive. The "families" are slices cozy in boxes, which is the thing he protects. Then he reads the ticket names as aliases and says it goes "all the way to the top." The top of a government is the capital, so Washington. Phil goes because Washington runs on long speeches and big empty rooms.

**COMP deep**
- DEEP: You weren't lying, boss. Something came up out of the storm drain. Some kind of mutant.
- DEEP: It chased me three blocks. I don't do three blocks. Is any of this worth it?

**COMP oldtimer** (thump)
- OLD: **THAT** is why we do this!
- OLD: So that tonight, somewhere out there, a family sleeps warm!
- OLD: Eight to a box. Shoulder to shoulder. Lid down. Cozy. They will never know your name.
- OLD: One nation, under cheese. Eight slices. **Indivisible!**

**COMP board**
- OLD: Now the tickets. K8lyn. Cat Lynn. Kæthlynn. Tell me that is a coincidence.
- NY: Could just be a popular name, boss.
- OLD: Or **aliases**. A new disguise at every door. This goes **all the way to the top**.

**COMP jumbo**
- OLD: And where is the top? The capital. Phil A. Buster. Washington is desks and speeches. You'll blend right in.
- JUMBO: I accept with humility, and shall begin with a statement of no more than four hours.
- OLD: Go.

### Scene 4: Jumbo returns, then Naples

**LOGIC:** "Original Italian" is printed on every box in every city. The Old Timer reads it as a signature, and a signature has a hometown. Everything pizza begins in Naples, so they go back to the start. Contessa is flattered and half-skeptical, which gives the leap a human face.

**COMP jumbo**
- JUMBO: Mr. Chairman, I rise before you a fraction of my former self. A **large** fraction, but a fraction.
- JUMBO: On the subject of the boxes. Every box in the District says **Original Italian.** Every one.
- DEEP: Mine too. I figured it was a font.

**COMP naples**
- NAPLES: Is on the box because is **true**. Original. Italian. I am the original!
- OLD: Two words, on every box, in every city. A signature. And a signature has a hometown.
- OLD: Where does it all begin, Contessa?
- NAPLES: ...Napoli. Finalmente.
- OLD: Then we go back to the beginning. **Naples.**

### Scene 5: Battered team, then Long Beach

**LOGIC:** The Old Timer's bridge is mundane, and the game never validates it. After twelve misspelled orders, the next three tickets are all in Long Beach, and the last one has the name spelled correctly: K-A-T-E-L-Y-N. His reading is that she dropped the disguise because it is her own house. The note says "one of each, it's a party," and he reads that as them knowing the roster. Then all four ask him to come along, and the stale line lands as the payoff.

**COMP wide**
- OLD: Look at you. Look at what is left of you.
- OLD: Good. Beaten up means close. I went back through every ticket.

**COMP board**
- OLD: Twelve orders. Twelve spellings. K8lyn to Qatelyn. Twelve disguises.
- OLD: Then at midnight, three more print. All Long Beach. And the last one is spelled **right**. K-A-T-E-L-Y-N.
- JUMBO: Perhaps a person may spell her own name, sir.
- OLD: Twelve disguises. Then one real name. You only drop the disguise at **home**.
- NAPLES: ...Mamma mia. That is almost logical.

**COMP wide**
- OLD: The note says, "One of each. It's a party." One of each of **you**. They know our roster.
- OLD: One slice won't do it. This takes all of you. Every crumb.
- JUMBO: A point of order, sir. Will you be joining us in the field?
- NAPLES: Sì. After all this, you come, yes?

**COMP oldtimer**
- OLD: Me? Kid, I was in the oven before your grandparents were **dough**.
- OLD: I'm too stale for this shit. [T]

## 7. Lines I am proudest of

1. **"Twelve disguises. Then one real name. You only drop the disguise at home."** The Long Beach bridge is mundane, clearly his reading and not proof. It turns the correctly spelled order into a clue, which is the one thing the script needed there.
2. **"Most come back with a report. The best don't come back at all. That is deep undercover."** This gives the premise a working logic. Silence is how he reads success, and the player reads the same silence as something else.
3. **"I would like to yield my time to myself."** It is a Phil joke that works on first meeting and gets funnier when the Old Timer cuts it off.
4. **Stan's "Second." / "Second City." / "...Fine."** It carries the logic (why Chicago), the character (a grudge) and the pause rhythm in three beats across boxes.
5. **"Me? Kid, I was in the oven before your grandparents were dough." / "I'm too stale for this shit."** The first box is the setup and the second is the punchline, so the swear lands at the end of the whole story.

## Departures from Tommy's notes

- **"I call them The Order":** this stays, but it now follows from the tickets. "Someone is pulling the strings" comes first, then "every vanishing starts with an order," then the name.
- **"Get in close, and find out who":** I moved this to the end of the New York scene, where it is the order Vinny gets sent out on. I also changed the last line to "who."
- **Central Park:** the customer is now the Pigeon Lady, with no bench in the line or the instruction.
- **"KATELYN™":** the final customer name is now plain "Katelyn," spelled right. Scene 5 depends on that.
- **Dropped:** all the housekeeping lines, "They told you this was undercover work. They lied," and CITY_DONE.
- **Ordering:** the Old Timer's sentences about a "family" and "Indivisible" are my own. They are the speech Tommy asked for in the repair outline, not his wording.
- **Comp count:** scene 2 has more comps than the usual three because the corkboard pan counts as one composition. Scene 1 runs about 25 lines, as the opening is allowed to.
