# Pizza Runner script pass: adventure-game room

**Departures from Tommy's notes**
- **Deep undercover:** it is a new rule, not a gag. Going quiet means you got in close, so a slice that stops calling is a success to the Old Timer. It pays off in NY (Vinny's missing slice), in CITY_LOST (the tape) and in "last slice".
- **Order wording:** "Someone is pulling the strings" is kept. "The Order" stays his named theory, with the added beat "Nobody else does. Yet."
- **Central Park:** the bench is gone. The customer is now the Pigeon Lady, so no line depends on a bench or a seat.
- **Long Beach bridge:** it rests on spelling, not string alone. It is his reading, not proof (see scene 5).
- **Opening:** it runs long, as allowed. The scene 2 pan beats push it to about 14 lines. Trim candidates are marked in scene 2.
- **Old Timer's name:** the speaker label is still "Old Timer" until Tommy picks one (section 2).

## 1. Voice sheet

**The Old Timer**
- **Voice:** clipped drill-sergeant noir. Every fact is "intel", and every scrap of silence is "proof".
- **Want:** find The Order before he is down to nothing.
- **Tic:** he answers every doubt with a bigger theory. When a recruit is truly rattled, he drops the volume and gets gentle for exactly one line.

**Vinny Two-Fold (New York)**
- **Voice:** fast, loud, a little hoarse. He drops g's and says "a" for "of" ("a nothin'", "some a me").
- **Want:** to be seen as unflappable.
- **Tic:** a brag, then a flinch in the very next sentence. He folds for nobody (and his name is Two-Fold).

**Big Stan (Chicago)**
- **Voice:** flat, short, deadpan. He says the least possible, then one sentence too many about casseroles.
- **Want:** to be left alone, and for nobody to say "casserole".
- **Tic:** "...Fine." He is the only one who never raises his voice. He just gets slower.

**La Contessa (Naples)**
- **Voice:** imperious, proud, Italian rhythm ("Is bigger now. Why is it bigger?"). She doubts that anyone outside Napoli knows food.
- **Want:** respect, certified in writing.
- **Tic:** quotes her ninety seconds in the fire. She is offended by ketchup in any form.

**Phil A. Buster (Washington, DC)**
- **Voice:** a senator mid-speech. He uses procedure ("point of order", "let the record show"), and every sentence is three clauses long.
- **Want:** the floor, and to keep it.
- **Tic:** he yields his time to himself. He is structurally unsound and keeps announcing it as a "budget."

## 2. Old Timer name candidates

1. **Sergeant Gus Overbake.** Recommended. He was left in the oven too long, which is also why he is angry. It sounds like a sergeant, and it does not spend the "stale" punchline early.
2. **Chief Al Forno.** "From the oven." It is a real pizza term, and it echoes the Naples door line "Il forno sa."
3. **Captain Day-Old.** The best joke, but it eats the "too stale" payoff. Use it only if the stale line moves.
4. **Lieutenant Hank Reheat.** A tough-guy name with a sad secret: he has been microwaved.
5. **Sarge Provolone.** Soft and cheesy name with a hard mustache. "Sarge Prov" fits a text box label.

## 7. Proudest lines

1. **"Nothing honest announces itself." / "Honest things announce themselves **constantly**. This is Italy."** The Old Timer's paranoid rule and Contessa's rebuttal land as one beat, and the scene still never debunks him.
2. **"That is not a name. That is a cover with a typo."** It makes the alias joke obey real logic: nobody spells their own name six ways, so the Old Timer's leap is wrong but sounds like a deduction.
3. **"Does it end, or did you run out?" / "They would expect that question."** It keeps the string joke from the old Long Beach scene. It gives Stan a skeptical aside that the Old Timer deflects, so nothing is debunked.
4. **"The one who has not called? **Deep undercover.** Be proud."** It repairs Tommy's weak "deep undercover" line with a premise that works: silence is the Old Timer's proof of success. It also sets up the tape on the board, the "last slice" returns and "deep" in the delivery line.
5. **"Me? I would love to, kid. I have been in this box a long time." / "I'm too stale for this shit."** The sincere beat gets one line, and the swear is the release. It saves the punchline for the cut to Long Beach.
