# Table-read notes: the Springfield room

## Scene 1: Opening to New York

**Best beats**
- **Order wording:** cop-show room, "Printed right on the ticket. That's how good they are." The name comes from something ordinary on the ticket, and "they aren't even hiding it" is the Old Timer's proof. That is the Dispatch tone: the player sees the hole and nobody points at it. It beats my "I came up with it myself. Nobody else says it. That's how deep it goes", which is only a boast. Adventure's "Nobody else does. Yet." is the best tag if we want it short.
- **Deep undercover:** my lid-and-tape rule ("Under a cover. Lid down, tape on. Nobody asks a box what's inside.") and Nintendo's "Most come back with a report. The best don't come back at all." are both good. Together they make the rule sinister and funny without saying why. Adventure's "A slice that stops calling is deep undercover" is the cleanest statement of the rule. I'd use the Nintendo line to set the rule and keep my tape for CITY_LOST.
- **Contessa interrupt:** mine, "Pipe down, Contessa. I'm getting to the string." It puts her "Mamma mia" right after "vanishing all over the world", which is what she's reacting to. Nintendo's "In Napoli we send postcards" is funnier but needs the silence rule to come first.
- **Phil intro:** adventure, "Three names, because one would not hold him. Structural integrity, none." The name joke has a premise. The cop room's "Only a senator needs all three" doesn't, because senators have two names.
- **Vinny intro:** adventure, "Folds in half. Never under pressure." It sets up "I fold for nobody." Pair it with my "Lid's open. Get in the box." as the answer to "Wait, what?"
- **Stan:** all four rooms wrote the same casserole exchange, and it works.

**Doesn't work**
- Adventure's "Plain cheese, every one of you. Nobody suspects plain cheese." Stan is deep dish and Contessa is margherita, so the premise fails on the first roster card.
- Cop-show's "Nobody looks up" as the reason for New York. It is a reason, but it contradicts the loud-slice joke in mine and Vinny's own character.
- My Phil bit "I entered that city whole. The record shows nothing. It is classified." That belongs in Scene 4, and it is padding here.

## Scene 2: New York to Chicago

**Best beats**
- **Vinny's return:** the [T] lines, plus the cop room's "I got my picture took four times." It plants the board pan.
- **Skeptic aside:** mine, "Has anybody tried? Provin' it?" / "That's how they get you." The Old Timer wins without debunking anything, and the line can come back later.
- **Why Chicago:** adventure, "Orders print at midnight, and I read them before the cook does." That is a real motive. Then Nintendo's "Second." / "Second City." / "...Fine." gives Stan his grievance, and I'd fold in my "I'm a dish. I don't dig." / "Dig anyway."
- **Board pan:** nobody beat the shared Teen and Turtle lines. All of them are already in the game, so we only need to trim.

**Doesn't work**
- Cop-show's "Dig up whatever else The Order is hiding." It is a pun with no motive.
- Nintendo's "Agents of The Order" tagged onto teenagers before the Old Timer has said the rule. Put the rule first, as the cop room and I did.

## Scene 3: Deep Dish to DC

**Best beats**
- **Speech:** Nintendo, "One nation, under cheese. ... **Indivisible!**" It is the only line that actually delivers the pseudo-patriotic speech Tommy asked for.
- **The number:** I cut "eight" from the speech because city pies are six-slice and the finale is eight. Adventure and cop-show both kept "Eight slices... Little ones by the crust." That is funny but contradicts the six-slice pies. I'd use "Indivisible" with no number.
- **Aliases:** adventure, "Nobody spells their own name six ways. That is not a name. That is a cover with a typo." Concession: my "Popular. Like a password is popular" is muddy, because popular passwords are common, so it half-supports Vinny. Adventure's version has a real premise, and it makes the Long Beach payoff work.
- **Why DC:** cop-show, "Phil, nobody can turn you away. You're the size of a door." Nintendo's "Washington is desks and speeches. You'll blend right in." is the runner-up. These give Phil a motive.

## Scene 4: Phil to Naples

**Best beats**
- **The button:** adventure, "Nothing honest announces itself." / "Honest things announce themselves **constantly**. This is Italy." It is the best exchange in any draft. Contessa pushes back, which makes her a suspect and doesn't debunk him.
- **Phil's motive:** cop-show, "But I read every lid in the capital." Phil reads the fine print, so he is the one who would notice.
- **Deep Dish:** Nintendo, "Mine too. I figured it was a font."
- **Contessa's exit:** Nintendo, "...Napoli. Finalmente." My "Everybody knows where I'm from. I tell them." is the better Contessa line, so keep both.

**Doesn't work**
- Cop-show's "Someone puts **my** name on a box?" The box says "Original Italian", not her name. The joke is off premise.
- Nintendo's "A large fraction, but a fraction" is a good gag but fights Tommy's liked "fraction of my former self". Keep the liked line and let the large-fraction gag go, or use it as an alt.

## Scene 5: Long Beach

**Best bridge:** Nintendo's. "Twelve orders. Twelve spellings... You only drop the disguise at **home**." It follows from Scene 3's "cover with a typo", it is plainly his reading and not proof, and the stakes are clear. I'm retiring my "most honest customer in America, or she wrote the others' names". It is clever, but it gives him two theories and no reason to pick Long Beach. Cop-show's "Nobody spells it right unless it's **theirs**" is the runner-up.

**Steals and alts**
- **The ticket note:** Nintendo's "One of each. It's a party." It plants the final "Pizza party!" and explains why all four go. It is new content, so Tommy should be told.
- **Skeptic aside:** Phil's "Perhaps a person may spell her own name, sir." is in voice. My alt for the reply is to echo Scene 2: OLD: "Spelling is how they get you."
- **Stan:** adventure's "Does it end, or did you run out?" is a good callback to the string. Use it only if Phil doesn't take the aside, because one skeptic is enough.
- **The stale line:** Nintendo's setup, "I was in the oven before your grandparents were **dough**." Mine adds Stan: "You never leave the building." / "I left the building." / "When?" / "...1987." Use mine as the setup, then the [T] line.

**Doesn't work**
- Cop-show's "Somebody has to mind the board." It is fine, but it doesn't earn the swear.
- Adventure's "string ends at one pin" is muddled imagery.
